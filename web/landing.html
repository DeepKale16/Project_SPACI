SPACI ARCHITECTURE

Catch what's left behind.

SPACI is an autonomous space debris detection and net-capture machine. It detects tumbling debris, calculates an intercept path, aims with a pan-tilt gimbal, and captures targets in a tethered nylon net.

Built and proven first as a ground demonstrator to evaluate sensors, pneumatic ejection, and ballistic guidance without flight launch risks.

Proven first as a ground demonstrator. Orbital capture concept shown not to scale.

A crowded orbit, and nothing to clean it up.

PERCEPTION & TRACKING

LiDAR ranging and camera vision locate the tumbling target.

Optical cameras classify object taxonomy while pulsed LiDAR estimates 3D metric coordinates. A pure-NumPy 6-state 3D Kalman filter tracks position and velocity vectors while filtering spurious sensor noise.

CHANNEL: RGB SENSOR + BOUNDING BOX ILLUSTRATIVE PIPELINE
BALLISTIC GUIDANCE & LAUNCH

Four pneumatic ejectors deploy the tethered net.

The pan-tilt mechanism slews to the lead intercept angle computed by a closed-form quartic ballistic solver. High-pressure pneumatic barrels fire corner projectile slugs that pull open a high-tensile nylon net across the deployment corridor.

Integrated permanent magnets assist corner slug adhesion when encountering ferrous fragments, maintaining envelope integrity during initial contact.

DRAWSTRING CINCH & WINCH

The drawstring cinches tight, and the winch reels in the catch.

Upon wrapping the tumbling debris, perimeter drawstrings pull the mouth of the net shut, locking the captured object inside. The motorized winch drum winds the high-tensile Dyneema tether back to the chassis.

Kinematic momentum transfer is damped through tension regulation, preventing recoil against the capture vehicle.

TEST STRATEGY

Why test on the ground first.

Space debris cannot be captured directly from Earth's surface. A ground testbed validates actuator timing, sensor fusion latency, net opening physics, and safety interlocks at a fraction of the cost of an orbital launch.

Ground Demonstrator ACTIVE IMPLEMENTATION
  • Stationary testbed mounting pan-tilt gimbal and pneumatic net ejectors
  • Compensates for 1-G terrestrial gravity in the ballistic trajectory solver
  • Tests physical debris mockups of varying density and geometry in a capture cage
  • Zero risk to operational space assets, rapid iterative firmware cycles
  • Empirically measures net aperture, flight time, and miss-distance error
Orbital Concept FUTURE MISSION TARGET
  • Free-flying spacecraft bus operating in microgravity environment
  • Zero-G parabolic trajectory calculations without aerodynamic drag
  • RCS thruster translation for rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO)
  • Radiation-hardened avionics, space-qualified solar power generation
  • De-orbits captured debris via controlled atmospheric re-entry burn

ENGINEERING SUBSYSTEMS

Interdisciplinary engineering subsystems.

The demonstrator unites precision mechanical hardware, power electronics, deterministic embedded firmware, and real-time computer vision.

Mechanical

4-barrel pneumatic launcher, high-pressure accumulator manifold, motorized winch spool, carbon-fiber structural chassis, and corner slug geometry.

Electronics

24V/12V dual-rail power distribution, solid-state solenoid drivers, optical encoder feedback, and isolated emergency cut-off bus.

Embedded Control

Microcontroller orchestrating gimbal slewing, pressure monitoring, safety gating, and telemetry serialization at sub-millisecond loop intervals.

AI and Vision

Dedicated neural detector, time-of-flight LiDAR integration, pure-NumPy 3D Kalman trajectory estimation, and quartic ballistic intercept solver.

Safety, Transparency and Aerospace Education

Debris removal involves complex orbital dynamics: unregulated impulses risk triggering catastrophic breakups under NASA-STD-8719.14C criteria (energy exceeding 40 J/g). SPACI serves as an open research and educational demonstrator to train students and aerospace researchers in autonomous computer vision, safety-interlocked GNC, and active debris removal methods before committing flight hardware.
