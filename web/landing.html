Space-debris capture machine

Catch what's left behind.

SPACI is a machine that detects moving debris, aims at it automatically and captures it in a tethered net. We build and prove it first as a ground demonstrator.

The problem

A crowded orbit, and nothing to clean it up.

Old satellites, spent rocket stages and fragments keep circling the Earth at orbital speed. Every piece is a collision risk, and each collision makes more pieces.

~47,000objects tracked in orbit
~1.5 Mdebris pieces larger than 1 cm
660+break-ups and fragmentation events

Source: ESA Space Debris Office, DISCOS statistics, updated 17 Nov 2026. Figures are approximate.

Step 1 and 2 / Detect and track

Two sensors, one track.

LiDAR and cameras on the machine find the debris and estimate its position. Each new frame updates how fast it moves and where it is heading.

Step 3 and 4 / Aim and capture

Four ejectors. One net.

The pan-tilt mount points the capture system at the target. Four pneumatic ejectors deploy the tethered nylon net, and magnets help hold ferrous debris.

Pan-tilt aim 4 ejectors Magnets

Step 5 / Retrieve

Close the net. Bring it in.

A drawstring closes the net around the catch and the winch reels the tether back to the machine.

The AI and vision pipeline

From sensor data to an aim point.

Sense

LiDAR returns and camera frames arrive together.

Detect

A vision model finds debris in the camera image.

Locate

LiDAR range turns the detection into a 3D position.

Predict

Tracking over time estimates speed and direction.

Aim

The pan-tilt controller points at where the target will be.

Ground and orbit

Why test on the ground first.

Ground demonstrator

  • Built and tested in a lab, at small scale.
  • Uses simulated debris that moves.
  • Safe, cheap and repeatable, so the same capture can be run many times.
  • Proves detection, aiming and net capture work together.

Orbital machine (concept)

  • Flies near real debris, not from the ground.
  • Faces microgravity and orbital speeds that a lab cannot reproduce.
  • Needs the same detect, aim and capture chain to work.
  • The ground results show whether the method is worth taking further.

Subsystems

Built across four disciplines.

Mechanical

Pan-tilt mount, ejector frame, net, magnets, winch and the moving debris simulator.

Electronics

Sensor interfaces, valve and motor drive, and power for the whole system.

Embedded control

Real-time control of pan-tilt aiming, ejector timing and the winch.

AI and vision

Detection, tracking and path prediction from LiDAR and camera data.

Safety and education

A safe place to study space technology.

The demonstrator is built and tested on the ground, at laboratory scale. It does not go to orbit. Simulated debris lets researchers study detection and capture safely, and every trial is measured the same way so results can be compared and repeated.
