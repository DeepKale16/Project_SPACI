🔬 Scientific Research: Orbital Hazard Assessment & Satellite State Categorization

SPACI 2.0 implements orbital collision risk formulations derived directly from international spaceflight standards: NASA-STD-8719.14C (Limiting Orbital Debris), ESA MASTER-8 (Meteoroid & Space Debris Terrestrial Reference), and IADC-02-01 (Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines).

🛰️ 1. How Spacecraft Are Categorized: Active vs. Inactive / Defunct

In aerospace surveillance, determining whether a detected satellite is actively controlled or defunct is vital for on-orbit rendezvous, Active Debris Removal (ADR), and collision avoidance:

Useful / Active Spacecraft
  • Attitude Stabilization: Controlled 3-axis stabilization via reaction wheels or thrusters. Rotational tumble rate is negligible (ω < 1°/s).
  • Operational Beacon: Active telemetry transponders and station-keeping orbital maneuvers.
  • Examples in SPACI: CHEOPS, PROBA-2, PROBA-3 CSC/OCS, SOHO, XMM-Newton, LISA Pathfinder.
  • Directive: Maximum preservation priority. Avoid any maneuvers that obstruct mission operations.
Inactive / Defunct Satellites
  • Uncontrolled Tumble: Solar radiation torque and gravity-gradient forces induce uncontrolled tumble (ω > 5°/s to 30°/s).
  • Structural Integrity: Retains mechanical bus integrity, solar arrays, and thruster blocks, but without power or propellant.
  • Examples in SPACI: SMART-1 (lunar demonstrator), Earth Observation Sat-1.
  • Directive: Eligible for On-Orbit Servicing (OOS), robotic refueling, or ADR capture if tumble is within robotic limits (ω < 5°/s).
Inert Fragmentation Debris
  • Non-Intact Fragments: Shards from catastrophic hypervelocity collisions, rocket stage explosions, or paint flakes.
  • High Area-to-Mass (HAMR): Chaotic atmospheric drag perturbations and unpredictable orbital decay lifetimes.
  • Examples in SPACI: Trackable debris fragments, Double Start ambiguity objects.
  • Directive: Catalog and track. Priority target for laser ablation or drag sail deorbiting.

⚡ 2. The SPACI 2.0 Multi-Factor Risk Assessment Formula

A pure computer vision bounding box cannot quantify kinetic threat without physics grounding. SPACI 2.0 calculates a continuous risk index R ∈ [0.0, 1.0]:

// Single-Frame Optical Risk Equation:
R_static = 0.55 · S(r_A) + 0.30 · P_threat + 0.15 · (1.0 - 0.25 · c) + Δ_telemetry

// Multi-Frame Temporal Video Tracking Equation:
R_video = 0.35 · S(r_A) + 0.35 · T(σ²_AR) + 0.20 · P_threat + 0.10 · (1.0 - 0.25 · c) + Δ_telemetry

Component Breakdown:

  • S(r_A) — Optical Angular Size Proxy: Derived from pinhole camera projection Ω = A_c / R². Scaled as S(r_A) = min(1.0, 3.9 · √r_A). Objects covering ≥6.5% of sensor FOV saturate at maximum size hazard.
  • T(σ²_AR) — Rotational Tumble Hazard: Computed over a 30-frame rolling window of bounding box aspect ratio variance: σ²_AR = Var(w_t / h_t). Scaled as T = min(1.0, 7.5 · σ²_AR). Fast tumbling expands the dynamic swept collision cross-section.
  • P_threat — Inherent Taxonomy Threat Prior: Fragment debris = 0.85, Defunct satellite bus = 0.50, Active scientific satellite = 0.15.
  • c — Epistemic Confidence Penalty: Compensates for AI model uncertainty: lower detection confidence adds a safety uncertainty margin.
  • Δ_telemetry — Flight Telemetry Integration:
    • Hypervelocity impact scaling: 0.15 · min(1.0, v_rel / 8000 m/s)
    • ESA MASTER-8 crowded shell penalty: +0.06 if orbital altitude is between 400 km and 900 km.
